Five things I've learned leading in-house brand design at a bank

Tanjina Sultana Camelia May 10, 2026 · 4 min read

People talk about brand design like it's an art project. Inside a bank, it's an infrastructure project. A logo lives on signage, debit cards, ATM screens, branch posters, internal slide decks, regulatory disclosures, event backdrops, and a hundred WhatsApp avatars no one approved. Five lessons from doing this for real, day after day.

1. The brand is the boring template

The most-used asset in any week is never the campaign poster — it's the slide template someone pulls at 4pm before a meeting. If that template is on-brand by default, the brand stays consistent without anyone "doing branding." If it isn't, no campaign can rescue it. Invest there first.

2. Internal users are the real audience

Every customer-facing asset goes through three or four hands inside the building before a customer sees it. Designing for those internal hands — clear labels, sensible defaults, a "don't" page in the guidelines — is what makes consistency scale. Customers see the result; my real users are the people inside.

3. Banking trust whispers

It's tempting to chase the energetic, animated, big-personality look the rest of the industry is doing. In banking, the brand has to whisper trust — restrained type, generous whitespace, photographs that feel honest. Loudness reads as a red flag. Confidence reads as quiet.

4. Process is half the design

A request that arrives as "we need a poster by Friday" is rarely about the poster. Half my job is asking why upstream — what decision is being made, what the campaign is supposed to do — so the design solves the right problem. The hours I spend on the brief save the team days on the artwork.

5. Documentation outlasts you

The cleanest legacy a brand lead can leave is a guideline document that the next designer can pick up in a single afternoon and run with. That's more valuable than any single campaign. I work on the guidelines like they're a product.

In-house brand work doesn't move in show-reel moments. It moves in the small invisible decisions that hold a brand together quietly, week after week.

None of this is glamorous. All of it is what the work actually is.
